# Margin of Safety

[[Benjamin Graham]]'s central concept and arguably the single most important idea in investing. Buy at a price sufficiently below intrinsic value that even if your analysis is wrong, you don't lose much.

## The Principle

> "The purpose of the margin of safety is to render the forecast unnecessary." — Benjamin Graham

You will be wrong about your estimates. The margin of safety ensures that being wrong doesn't mean being ruined.

If you estimate a stock is worth $100:
- Buy at $95 → almost no margin of safety (your estimate better be right)
- Buy at $70 → 30% margin (you can be significantly wrong and still be okay)
- Buy at $50 → 50% margin (even a terrible estimate leaves room)

The larger the uncertainty, the larger the margin should be.

## Beyond Stocks

Margin of safety is a universal risk principle that applies everywhere:

| Domain | Margin of Safety |
|--------|-----------------|
| Value investing | Buy below intrinsic value |
| Engineering | Bridges built to 3x expected load |
| [[Position sizing]] | Bet less than Kelly says |
| Trading | Stop loss = predefined margin of error |
| Crypto | Only invest what you can lose entirely |
| Life | Emergency fund = financial margin of safety |

## Relationship to Other Concepts

- **[[Antifragility]]** (Taleb): Margin of safety makes you robust; antifragility makes you benefit from the same shocks. Margin of safety is the floor; antifragility is the upside.
- **Minimax** ([[john-von-neumann]]): Margin of safety = minimize your maximum possible loss. Same principle, different language.
- **Kelly Criterion** ([[kelly-criterion]]): Fractional Kelly is margin of safety applied to bet sizing — you bet less than optimal because your estimate of "optimal" is wrong.
- **[[Reflexivity]]** ([[george-soros]]): In a reflexive environment, intrinsic value itself shifts. Graham assumed stable intrinsic value; Soros argues it moves. The margin needs to account for this.

## Why Traders Ignore It

In practice, margin of safety is boring. It means:
- Passing on most trades (not enough margin)
- Holding cash (opportunity cost)
- Missing rallies (waiting for cheaper prices that may not come)

This is why [[jesse-livermore]], [[three-arrows-capital]], and [[ftx-collapse]] blow up — they trade without margin, and when the estimate is wrong, there's nothing to absorb the error.
