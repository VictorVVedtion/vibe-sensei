# Reflexivity

[[George Soros]]'s foundational theory. Markets are not passive mirrors — they are active participants that shape the reality they're supposed to reflect.

## The Theory

Traditional economics assumes markets tend toward equilibrium: prices reflect fundamentals, and when they deviate, arbitrage pushes them back. Soros argues this is wrong.

**Reflexivity** = a two-way feedback loop between:
1. **Cognitive function**: Participants try to understand reality (form expectations)
2. **Manipulative function**: Participants' actions change reality (based on those expectations)

Prices influence fundamentals, which influence prices. The loop can be self-reinforcing (boom) or self-defeating (bust).

## Examples

### Housing Bubble (2003–2008)
1. Rising home prices → banks lend more (collateral is worth more)
2. More lending → more buying → prices rise further
3. Higher prices → looser lending standards (prices "can't fall")
4. Feedback loop continues until the gap between price and reality is unsustainable
5. One break → cascade collapse ([[ltcm-collapse]] at macro scale)

### Crypto Bull Runs
1. Token price rises → project treasury grows → more hiring/marketing
2. More development → more adoption → price rises further
3. Higher price → more speculation → more liquidity → higher price
4. [[do-kwon]]'s LUNA/UST was reflexivity at its most extreme — the token's value backed the stablecoin whose demand drove the token's value

### Currency Pegs
1. Market doubts peg → sells currency → reserves drain
2. Draining reserves → more doubt → more selling
3. Self-fulfilling prophecy until the peg breaks
4. This is exactly what [[george-soros]] exploited in 1992 against the British pound

## Why It Matters for Trading

- **Trend following works** because reflexivity creates extended trends beyond "fair value"
- **Bubbles are tradeable** — ride the reflexive boom, exit before the reflexive bust
- **Mean reversion has limits** — in a reflexive environment, "too expensive" can get more expensive
- **Leverage amplifies reflexivity** — [[three-arrows-capital]] and [[ltcm-collapse]] show what happens when leveraged positions interact with reflexive price action
