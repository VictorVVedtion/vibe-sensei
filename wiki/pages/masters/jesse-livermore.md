# Jesse Livermore

**Rarity**: Legendary | **Archetype**: Trend/Momentum | **Era**: 1877–1940

> "The market is never wrong, opinions are."

The Boy Plumber of Wall Street. Started trading at 14 in bucket shops, made and lost multiple fortunes. His life is the definitive parable of speculation — brilliant pattern recognition paired with catastrophic risk management failures.

## Trading Philosophy

Livermore was a **pure tape reader** who evolved into a macro trend follower. His core beliefs:

1. **Markets move in trends** — Don't fight the tape. The trend is your friend until it ends.
2. **Let winners run, cut losers fast** — His "pivotal point" system defined entries; his discipline (when he had it) defined exits.
3. **The big money is in the big swings** — He didn't scalp. He waited for the setup, then went big.
4. **Cash is a position** — Sitting out is a valid trade. Most of the time, do nothing.

## Key Trades

- **1907 Panic**: Shorted the market ahead of the crash. J.P. Morgan personally asked him to stop selling. Made $1M in a single day (equivalent to ~$30M today).
- **1929 Crash**: His greatest trade. Built a massive short position through the summer of 1929, netting ~$100M ($1.5B+ today) as the market collapsed. He read the euphoria and bet against it.
- **Cotton and Wheat**: Early trend-following trades in commodities that built his initial fortune.

## Fatal Flaws

- **Overleveraging**: Made fortunes but also went bankrupt multiple times. No [[position-sizing]] discipline when conviction was high.
- **Emotional trading**: His own rules were clear, but he violated them repeatedly — especially after personal losses.
- **No risk floor**: Unlike [[warren-buffett]] who protects capital above all, Livermore had no maximum drawdown rule.

## Relevance to Vibe Sensei

Livermore embodies the tension between brilliance and discipline. As a guardian, he warns against overleveraging and emotional revenge trading. His ghost watches for the same patterns that destroyed him — doubling down on losers, ignoring stop losses, letting ego override the tape.
