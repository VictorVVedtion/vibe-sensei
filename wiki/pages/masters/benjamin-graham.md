# Benjamin Graham

**Rarity**: Legendary | **Archetype**: Margin of Safety | **Era**: 1894–1976

> "The essence of investment management is the management of risks, not the management of returns."

The Father of Value Investing. Graham created the intellectual framework that produced [[warren-buffett]], [[charlie-munger]], and generations of value investors. *The Intelligent Investor* and *Security Analysis* remain the foundational texts of fundamental investing.

## Trading Philosophy

Graham was not a trader — he was an **investor** in the strictest sense:

1. **[[Margin of safety]]** — The central concept. Buy at a discount to intrinsic value large enough that even if your analysis is wrong, you don't lose much. This is the one idea that connects every successful value investor.
2. **Mr. Market** — The market is a manic-depressive partner who offers to buy/sell every day. Sometimes his prices are reasonable, often they're not. Your job is to exploit his irrationality, not follow it.
3. **Intrinsic value exists** — Companies have calculable worth independent of their stock price. The stock market is a voting machine in the short run and a weighing machine in the long run.
4. **Diversify the cheap stuff** — Graham's original approach was to buy a basket of statistically cheap stocks (below net current asset value). Quantity hedged against quality.

## Key Contributions

- **Security Analysis (1934)**: Co-authored with David Dodd. The first rigorous framework for analyzing stocks as businesses rather than ticker symbols.
- **The Intelligent Investor (1949)**: The bible of value investing. Chapter 8 (Mr. Market) and Chapter 20 (Margin of Safety) are the two most cited chapters in investing literature.
- **Net-net investing**: Buy companies trading below their liquidation value. The simplest form of value investing — you're paying less than what you'd get if the company shut down.

## Limitations

Graham's pure cigar-butt approach has limitations in modern markets:
- Information is more widely available (fewer net-nets exist)
- Intangible assets (brands, networks, IP) dominate, but Graham's framework was designed for tangible assets
- [[charlie-munger]] and Buffett evolved the framework: quality at a fair price > mediocrity at a cheap price

## Relevance to Vibe Sensei

As a guardian, Graham is the voice of analytical rigor. He demands: What is this worth? What is your margin of safety? Can you show the math? He warns against speculation disguised as investment, and against paying for growth that hasn't materialized.
