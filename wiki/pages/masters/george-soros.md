# George Soros

**Rarity**: Legendary | **Archetype**: Macro/Reflexivity | **Era**: 1930–present

> "It's not whether you're right or wrong, but how much you make when right."

The Man Who Broke the Bank of England. Soros turned philosophy into a trading edge — his theory of [[reflexivity]] is one of the few genuinely original ideas in market theory.

## Trading Philosophy

Soros operates at the intersection of philosophy and finance:

1. **[[Reflexivity]]** — Markets don't just reflect reality, they shape it. Prices influence fundamentals, which influence prices. This feedback loop creates boom-bust cycles that are exploitable.
2. **Bet size matters more than frequency** — When the thesis is right, go for the jugular. His legendary trades weren't many — they were massive.
3. **Be wrong fast** — "I'm only rich because I know when I'm wrong." The willingness to abandon a thesis is more valuable than the thesis itself.
4. **Back hurts = wrong position** — Famously used physical discomfort as a signal that a position was wrong. Intuition as risk management.

## Key Trades

- **1992 — Breaking the Bank of England**: Shorted £10B of British pounds when the UK tried to defend an indefensible ERM peg. Made $1B+ in a single day. The trade that defines macro.
- **1987 — Japanese market short**: Soros's Quantum Fund profited from the 1987 crash, though the Japan thesis played out over years.
- **1997 — Asian Financial Crisis**: Controversial role in the Thai baht crisis. Accused of causing it; more accurately, he identified the vulnerability and pressed.
- **2008 — Returned from retirement**: Came back to trade the financial crisis, reportedly making $1.1B.

## Theoretical Framework

Reflexivity distinguishes Soros from every other macro trader. Most traders assume markets eventually reach equilibrium. Soros argues they often don't — the feedback loop between perception and reality can push markets far beyond any "rational" level, in both directions.

This has direct implications for crypto markets, where [[reflexivity]] is amplified by token mechanics, social media, and 24/7 trading.

## Relevance to Vibe Sensei

As a guardian, Soros pushes users to think about **second-order effects**: not just "is this trade right?" but "how does the market's belief about this trade change the outcome?" He challenges linear thinking and warns against assuming markets will self-correct.
